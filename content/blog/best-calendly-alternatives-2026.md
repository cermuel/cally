## What to look for in a scheduling tool

Start with the scheduling job you need to solve. These questions usually reveal more than a generic “best tool” ranking:

1. **Which calendars must it check?** A scheduler should protect events on the calendars that represent your real commitments.
2. **How many meeting types do you need?** One booking option may be enough for an occasional call. Consultants and teams often need different durations and purposes.
3. **Does scheduling belong in another workflow?** Sales teams may need CRM records, lead routing, and follow-up automation. Independent professionals may prefer less machinery.
4. **Who needs to administer it?** Individual scheduling is simpler than pooled availability, round-robin assignment, permissions, and organisation-wide controls.
5. **What should the guest experience feel like?** Check the booking page on a phone, review the amount of information requested, and confirm that time zones and meeting details are clear.
6. **What does “free” actually include?** Look at event-type limits, calendar connections, branding, integrations, reminders, and team features—not just the headline price.

## 1. Cally

Cally is a focused meeting scheduler for creating a public booking page, defining meeting types, setting weekly availability, and checking Google Calendar before a guest chooses a time. After a booking, Cally creates the calendar event, adds a Google Meet link, and sends confirmation details to the host and guest.

The guest does not need a Cally account. A host can present multiple meeting options—each with its own name, description, duration, and availability—on one public page. This makes Cally a practical fit for independent professionals, creators, advisors, and small teams whose scheduling already revolves around Google Calendar.

### Where Cally stands out

- A simple public profile brings meeting types and host context together.
- Weekly availability and Google Calendar busy time both influence bookable slots.
- Confirmed bookings create Google Calendar events with Google Meet details.
- The product stays centred on scheduling rather than expanding into a full CRM.

### What to confirm

Cally currently presents itself as free to start with no credit card required, but its public product information does not yet define usage limits or paid-plan terms. Confirm those details before treating it as a long-term pricing commitment. Cally’s current calendar integration is specifically Google Calendar, so people who require Microsoft 365 or several calendar providers should evaluate another option.

## 2. Cal.com

Cal.com combines an individual scheduler with team and organisation features, a large integration surface, and open-source deployment options. Its [official pricing page](https://cal.com/pricing) lists a free individual plan with unlimited event types and calendar connections, while paid plans add collaborative scheduling, routing, analytics, administration, and enterprise controls.

That breadth makes Cal.com worth considering when flexibility is the main requirement. Technical organisations may appreciate APIs and deployment choices; growing teams may care more about round-robin scheduling, managed event types, or routing forms.

### Where Cal.com stands out

- Broad calendar and application integrations.
- Individual, team, organisation, and enterprise paths.
- Team scheduling capabilities such as shared availability and round robin.
- An open-source ecosystem for organisations that want more control.

### What to consider

Breadth can introduce setup and administration that a solo user does not need. Decide whether you will actually use the integrations, routing, payments, or team controls that distinguish the platform. Compare annual and monthly terms directly on the pricing page because displayed rates and discounts can change.

## 3. Google Calendar appointment schedules

Google Calendar includes appointment schedules for eligible accounts. According to [Google’s appointment schedule documentation](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/11608416), users can create a booking page, share or embed it, set availability, and block times that conflict with Calendar events. This can be the shortest path for someone who already spends the day in Google Calendar and does not want another scheduling dashboard.

Google states that a personal Google Account or Workspace Business Starter account can create a single booking page. Its [premium feature comparison](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/16287038) shows that features such as multiple schedules, automatic reminders, multi-calendar availability checks, payments, email verification, secondary calendars, and co-hosts depend on the account or subscription.

### Where Google Calendar stands out

- Scheduling lives directly in a familiar calendar product.
- Booked appointments appear in Google Calendar.
- Availability and conflict checking use the same ecosystem.
- A simple use case may not require a separate scheduling subscription.

### What to consider

The available feature set depends on the Google account type. Check the current eligibility table rather than assuming that a feature shown in a tutorial is included with your plan. Teams that need workflow automation outside Google, advanced routing, or a distinct branded scheduling product may prefer a dedicated tool.

## 4. HubSpot meeting scheduler

HubSpot’s scheduler is designed as part of a wider sales platform. Its [official meeting scheduler page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/sales/schedule-meeting) describes shareable booking links that connect with Google or Office 365 calendars and log activity within HubSpot’s CRM. It also highlights website embeds, group meeting links, and sales-oriented preparation and follow-up.

This is a strong fit when the meeting is one stage in a lead or customer workflow. If a team already uses HubSpot for contacts, sales activity, and email, keeping scheduling in the same record can be more valuable than choosing a standalone scheduler with a cleaner interface.

### Where HubSpot stands out

- Scheduling connects directly with CRM contacts and sales activity.
- Google and Microsoft calendar connections are supported.
- Booking links can sit inside sales emails, chat, and web pages.
- A free starting option is available, with advanced capabilities in paid Sales Hub editions.

### What to consider

HubSpot is a broader platform, and its value rises when you use that surrounding CRM. For a person who only wants an appointment link, the account structure and sales tooling may be more than necessary. Review the current Sales Hub packaging for the exact automation, team, and AI features you need.

## 5. Calendly

Calendly remains a reasonable option and is included here as the baseline many people already know. The goal of looking for an alternative is not to assume the familiar tool is bad; it is to check whether a different product fits a specific workflow better.

Calendly’s [official pricing page](https://calendly.com/pricing) lists a free plan with one event type and one calendar connection. The Standard plan adds unlimited event types, multiple calendar connections, reminders, automations, integrations, and payment connections, while higher plans focus on team scheduling, administration, and enterprise requirements.

### Where Calendly stands out

- A mature general-purpose scheduling product.
- Options for individual, team, and enterprise scheduling.
- A broad set of integrations and workflow features on paid plans.
- Familiar booking behaviour for many guests.

### What to consider

The free plan’s event-type and calendar limits may be decisive for people with several meeting formats. Paid features may be worthwhile if you need integrations, reminders, payments, or team controls, but a narrower tool can be easier when those capabilities are unnecessary.

## How to choose

Use a short real-world trial instead of comparing checkboxes indefinitely:

1. Create the two meeting types you schedule most often.
2. Connect the calendar that contains your genuine busy time.
3. Open the public booking page on a phone and complete a test booking as a guest.
4. Check the confirmation, calendar event, video link, cancellation path, and time zone.
5. Invite one colleague or client to test it without coaching.
6. Review the exact plan limits that would affect your next six months.

Choose **Cally** when you want a focused Google Calendar and Google Meet workflow with a personal public page. Choose **Cal.com** when broad integrations, open-source flexibility, or expanding team controls matter. Choose **Google Calendar appointment schedules** when native Calendar convenience is the priority. Choose **HubSpot** when scheduling should be part of a CRM-led sales process. Stay with or choose **Calendly** when its established integrations and paid workflow features match your needs.

No scheduler removes the need for thoughtful availability. Protect focus time, use clear meeting names, avoid unnecessary form fields, and make the next step obvious. The tool should reduce coordination—not create another system everyone has to manage.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free alternative to Calendly?

Yes. Cally is free to start, Cal.com lists a free individual plan, Google Calendar provides an appointment-schedule entry point for eligible accounts, and HubSpot offers a free meeting scheduler. “Free” covers different limits in each product, so compare event types, connected calendars, branding, integrations, and team features.

### Which option is best for Google Calendar?

Google Calendar appointment schedules provide the most native experience. Cally is a focused alternative for people who want a separate public profile and meeting-type experience while still using Google Calendar and Google Meet. Test both with the same real meeting workflow.

### Do invitees need an account?

For the tools discussed here, the intended booking flow generally lets a guest use a shared page without buying a scheduler seat. Providers may still use email verification, sign-in, or anti-spam checks in specific configurations, so test the exact settings you plan to use.

### Should a small business choose a CRM scheduler?

Choose a CRM-connected scheduler when booking data needs to trigger sales ownership, pipeline updates, sequences, or reporting. If the only job is to show availability and confirm a meeting, a focused scheduler is usually simpler.
